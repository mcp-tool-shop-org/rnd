## Summary

E5 asks, for each local 5090 model, the largest context window (`num_ctx`) that stays fully on the card, stays under
30500 MiB, and doesn't spill into system memory. The first complete run (`e5-2026-10-13b`, interlock d899ac0, clean
tree) gave three valid answers and three that aren't capacities yet:
- **three models are valid at their trained cap;**
- **two are lower bounds,** because a safety predictor stopped the search early;
- **one is a misfire** of the pre-registered spill rule, so it isn't a capacity.

A re-run of those three follows a dated amendment.

## Key points

| Model | Result | Status |
|---|---|---|
| mistral-small:24b | 32768 (trained cap) | valid: shared +82 MiB over the 2048 floor, prompt ratio 0.89 |
| qwen3:14b | 40960 (trained cap) | valid: +219 MiB, ratio 0.75 |
| qwen3:8b | 40960 (trained cap) | valid: +206 MiB, ratio 1.02 |
| gemma4:31b | ≥ 14336 | lower bound: the full-attention predictor said 38922 MiB at 16384, but the measured peaks rise about 30 MiB per 2k (sliding-window attention) |
| granite4.1:30b | ≥ 30720 | lower bound: predicted 38498 MiB at 32768, measured about +514 MiB per 2k (a hybrid model) |
| llama3.1:8b | 53248 under the pre-registered rule | rule misfire, not a capacity |

**Why llama3.1:8b is a misfire:**
- Its dedicated peak never passed about 24.2 GB of the card's 32.6 GB. The whole run's max, from the 5-second counter log, was 27.3 GB. So no capacity spill was possible.
- Shared usage grew about 4 KiB per token of context, which is host-side scaling, not a gigabyte-scale KV overflow.
- A 120k-token prompt processes more slowly than a 2k one, by the nature of attention.
- Measured against the 2048 floor, both gates therefore tripped on normal scaling.

**How spill is measured:**
- Each load reserves pinned host buffers (CUDA_Host) that Windows counts as shared GPU memory: about 352 MiB for qwen3:8b, and 1128 MiB for gemma4:31b, whose large vocabulary embedding sits on the host. That's why the baseline is the model's own floor window, not the idle card.
- Every model's floor passed the check that those buffers explain it.

**The amendment before the re-run** (dated in the spec):
- shared growth gates only when the dedicated peak is at or above abort_mib − 2048 (28452 MiB);
- the prompt-rate gate becomes a cliff test against the next lower confirmed window;
- the predictor uses measured peak-per-token slopes after two resident loads;
- the receipt reports "predicted" when the predictor stopped the search.

**Defects found on the way, all fixed before this run:**
- an adapter match that required exact equality;
- uncaptured subprocess output;
- a home path refused at write time after 70 minutes;
- a spill baseline that compared a window with itself.

The first three are listed in [[2026-10-12-test-fakes-hide-the-platform]].

## Studio relevance

For routing today: qwen3:8b and qwen3:14b can run their full 40960 window on the 5090, and mistral-small:24b its full
32768. gemma4:31b holds at least 14336 and granite4.1:30b at least 30720. llama3.1:8b's capacity is open until the
re-run. It is at least 53248, and the card's headroom suggests far more.

## Claims

- [verified] mistral-small:24b at 32768, and qwen3:14b and qwen3:8b at 40960, confirmed under the pre-registered rule with baseline_check ok (via: R&D's read of receipts/e5-2026-10-13b.jsonl, 2026-10-13)
- [verified] gemma4:31b's and granite4.1:30b's searches stopped on predicted_over, with measured peaks far below the predictions (via: R&D's read of the receipt's search calls, 2026-10-13)
- [verified] llama3.1:8b's dedicated peak stayed at or below about 24.2 GB at every window, and the run's max was 27.3 GB (via: R&D's read of the receipt and counters.csv, 2026-10-13)
