## Summary

Our d5c benchmark for language-model judges is generated by a program: triads of short fact sets with numbers and a
claim, one item per label (supported, unsupported, cannot tell). We audit the generator for "answer cues", single-item
features that predict the label without solving the item. Each repair of one cue moved it rather than removed it. We
asked a Gemini Deep Research run how to audit such a generator and how to state what an audit can rule out. The report
is `[aggregator]` AI output; a harvest clerk checked its main citations and recomputed its key arithmetic. Only public
demonstration seeds were described to it; no test items.

Its method advice matches the rules we had already fixed for the repair's acceptance (within-triad label shuffles,
fresh seeds fixed in advance, a probe penalty that scales with pool size, and a stated bound rather than a claim of
zero). It argues for disclosing a residual honestly, not for another repair round. Its theory of unavoidable leakage
overreaches.

## Key points

- **Two kinds of audit.** Top-down (the designer lists suspected heuristics and tests each) and bottom-up (train a
  model on a partial view, such as the claim alone, and see what it gets). Partial-input baselines exposed large
  artifacts in natural-language inference and fact-checking sets (Poliak et al. 2018; Schuster et al. 2019).
- **Learned probes need a penalty that scales with sample size.** A linear probe with a fixed per-sample penalty goes
  blind as the pool grows, because the null signal shrinks like the square root of the sample size. We had found
  exactly this, and rescaled our probe the same way.
- **Calibration.** Within-triad shuffling gives the exact null. Permutation p-values should be (b + 1)/(B + 1)
  (Phipson and Smyth 2010). At a 30-item slice, the family-wise bar across hundreds of features is stricter than a
  58% purity bar alone, so the purity number rarely binds.
- **Repair or disclose.** When the label is a function of bounded numbers, removing every cue is not possible in
  general; balancing one feature moves the lean into correlated ones. The honest output is an upper bound on what a
  restricted cheater can gain at a stated audit size.
- **How to state a pass.** Audit size; the smallest slice tested as a share of the pool; our own detection margin; a
  one-sided upper bound on a cheater's gain; and what was not tested.
- **Avoiding forking paths.** Separate development seeds, calibration seeds, one-shot acceptance seeds and release
  seeds, with a timestamped pre-registration locking the generator, audit code and thresholds.
- **What we had not asked.** Digit-length boundaries (99 to 100) as cues; the position of the deciding fact; scoring
  by whole triads, where a constant guesser scores 0% and a random one 3.7%.

## Studio relevance

### What it contradicts in our design (read first)

| Finding | Our design today | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Our learned-model acceptance line is noisy: on a clean generator it can fire by chance | The fresh-seed acceptance stops if the model gains 0.005 or more in accuracy on any of three pools | The line is unchanged. Its expected false-stop rate (about 1% to 10% over three pools, from our own null measurements) is stated in a dated addendum before any acceptance pool is drawn. Loosening it was considered and rejected, because a candidate had just failed it |
| The pass statement should carry the audit size, slice floor, detection margin, a cheater bound and untested scope | Our pass statements give the gate result | The outcome note carries all five, whichever way the acceptance lands |
| Tail-indicator columns help a linear probe see rare cues; digit-boundary and fact-position statistics are untested | Not in our battery | Optional additions; not adopted yet |
| A construction with no numeric leakage exists (balanced arrangements of entities) | Not our item form | Parked: a redesign with new cue surfaces, and the report never says how "cannot tell" items are built |

### Decisions and actionables

1. The acceptance line stays as written; its false-stop rate is now on record before the data.
2. The review of the repair's acceptance confirms that the learned model and the lookup use triad-grouped folds.
3. The outcome note adds the report's pass-statement items.

## Claims

- [verified] A hypothesis-only model reaches 69.00% on SNLI against a 34.28% majority baseline (via: Poliak et al. 2018, aclanthology S18-2023, harvest clerk, 2026-11-20)
- [verified] A claim-only model reaches 61.7% on FEVER, and a leading model drops from 81.8% to 58.7% on a symmetric test set (via: Schuster et al. 2019, arXiv 1908.05267, harvest clerk, 2026-11-20)
- [verified] At B = 2,000 permutations and 600 tests, the smallest attainable family-wise p from a rank-based min-p is about 0.26 (via: recomputed by the harvest clerk, 2026-11-20)
- [unverified] Every non-constant linear threshold function of the numbers leaks label information through some single summary (the report's reading of Chow's theorem; it holds only when class totals are stochastically ordered, which a class-invariant reserve breaks)
- [unverified] With 300 features at a family-wise 0.05, a 30,000-item pool detects a 1.37-point cue with 90% power (the report's figure; not shown or sourced)
